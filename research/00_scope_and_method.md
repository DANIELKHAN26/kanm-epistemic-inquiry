# Research scope and method

**Evidence cutoff:** 15 November 2026 (Australia/Sydney).

**Purpose:** Understand the historical Laputa model, identify which limitations are established and which have already been addressed by later work, and specify a credible path toward a more usable research model.
**Review type:** Targeted deep research, not a systematic review or a claim that all relevant literature has been exhausted.

## Working research question

How should a reproducible successor to Laputa represent collaborative, question-driven inquiry when evidence may be repeated, scope-limited, or dependent on other sources, and how do those mechanisms affect truth-tracking, calibration, inquiry cost, and the distribution of epistemic benefits?

## Subquestions

1. What does the published Laputa model and the historical software actually represent?
2. Which simplifying assumptions or implementation risks are explicit in the primary literature?
3. Which apparent gaps have already been addressed in related models, experiments, or theoretical work?
4. What combination of extensions would add clear scientific value without silently changing Laputa's assumptions?
5. What tests and validation evidence would be needed before describing a new implementation as a faithful port or a validated model?

## Search and screening approach

Searches prioritized original articles, publisher pages, author/institution repositories, the project host, and model-documentation/validation research. Search strands covered: Laputa's equations and software history; social-network topology and truth-tracking; repeated/shared evidence and testimony dependence; question/subquestion-based group inquiry; epistemic vigilance and domain competence; credibility injustice; and agent-based model documentation and validation. Secondary reference pages were used to discover primary work, not to establish claims where the primary source was available.

Included sources were retained when they directly addressed Laputa, one of its explicit assumptions, a proposed extension mechanism, or accepted practice for documenting and validating agent-based simulations. Preprints are labeled as such. The evidence does not support a universal ranking of venues, so “top-tier” is operationalized here as methodologically influential, peer-reviewed, primary, or directly relevant work rather than an impact-factor claim.

## Evidence labels used in this project

- **Verified fact:** directly stated in a primary source or official project record linked in [references](references.md).
- **Synthesis:** an interpretation drawn across sources; the inferential step is stated.
- **Candidate:** a proposed design or research contribution that still needs formalization and comparison with prior art.
- **Unverified:** not confirmed from accessible primary material.

## Scope boundaries

This review does not claim to establish that no competing or maintained implementation exists anywhere. It also does not establish human predictive validity for Laputa or for any proposed extension. Model reproduction, software parity, and empirical validation are separate claims and require separate evidence.
