## 1. Defects found in v0.2.0 and how they were fixed

Every item below was reproduced before it was fixed and now has a regression test.

**Documentation error found by the same review.** Section 2 had cited the HHO App. B worked value as evidence for the simultaneous schedule. It is not: with one report per source every schedule gives the same value. The section is corrected; the schedule claim rests on the source code alone.

**Remaining discretisation limit (documented, not a defect).** Once a single source has produced many more reports than there are grid cells, its posterior concentrates in one or two cells. The likelihood ratio for that source then saturates at about `log(x_top / (1 − x_top))`, roughly log(2·points), instead of continuing to grow. Use more `trust_points` for very long runs, or check that results are stable when the grid is refined. Unlike D16, this limit does not reverse the direction of evidence.

Outputs for a given seed differ from v0.2.0 because of:
- D5 (new random streams);
- D9–D10 (new discretisation);
- the new default schedule (section 2).

They differ from v0.3.0 only in runs affected by D16 (very long runs) or D17 (exact threshold ties, `T ∈ {0, 1}`). This is intentional and recorded in `model_variant`.

## 2. What the primary sources establish about the historical model

Sources read: Olsson (2011, 2013), Vallinder & Olsson (2014), Angere's 2010 preprint, Hahn, Hansen & Olsson (2020, Appendix B/C), Angere & Olsson (2017), the Laputa 1.6 manual, and the Laputa 1.6 C++ source from the official SourceForge archive (`src/Society.cpp`, `src/Inquirer.cpp`, `src/Trust.cpp`). The source was read only to specify behaviour; no code was copied or translated.

| Question | Finding | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Order of operations within a step | All inquiry results and messages at step *t* are generated from credences at the start of the step (`C_t`). Each agent combines them in one update; only then are all credences replaced (`Society::evolve`). This matches the papers' `C_{t+1}(p) = C_t(p ∣ ⋀ S^t)`. | Independence of credence and reliability | Stated as an explicit axiom, "Communication Independence" (Olsson 2013; Angere App. A; HHO App. C), and re-imposed at every step. Only a scalar credence and one marginal density per source are stored. | **Verified fact** |

**Consequences for KANM.**
- The default schedule is now `"simultaneous"`, the order of operations confirmed in the 1.6 source.
- The v0.2.0 order is kept as `"inquiry_then_messages"`.
- A new `assertion_rule="laputa_1_6"` reproduces the code's threshold behaviour for docking.
- The default stays `"published"`.
- KANM's `publish_only_after_inquiry` is **not** the 1.6 "new evidence" policy. That policy stores a per-link message and compares last-use and last-inquiry times. It is implemented separately, as `schedule="laputa_1_6"` with `evidence_policy`, and was docked in v0.4.0 (§7).

## 3. Laputa's update is a factorised approximation: prior art and what is new

### 3.1 The mathematics (verified by derivation and by brute-force tests)

Assume, as Laputa does:
- `p` is a binary proposition;
- source reliabilities `r_i` are a priori independent of `p` and of each other;
- each report is correct with probability `r_i`;
- reports are conditionally independent given `p` and the `r_i`.

Then, for any data:

```
P(p, r_1..r_k | data) = P(p | data) · ∏_i τ_i(r_i | p, data),
τ_i(r | p, data) ∝ τ_i⁰(r) · r^{a_i} (1-r)^{b_i},   τ_i(r | ¬p, data) ∝ τ_i⁰(r) · r^{b_i} (1-r)^{a_i},
```

Here `a_i` and `b_i` count source *i*'s positive and negative reports.

- **Sufficient statistic.** The exact posterior needs only two conditional densities per source, which is constant memory. It depends on the data only through the counts, so it is **order invariant**.
- **Where Laputa differs.** Laputa's rule keeps `P(p)` and the marginal `τ_i`. For one report per source the two rules give identical credences (test `test_one_report_per_source_equals_laputa_belief`).
- **Why it fails after that.** Once a source repeats, its reports are correlated through the shared unknown `r_i`. Laputa re-imposes independence and so treats each repeat as fresh evidence.
- **Size of the error.** With prior 0.5 and trust Beta(mean 0.6, concentration 8), ten consistent reports from one source give:
  - exact posterior 0.897 (log-odds 2.16);
  - Laputa 0.9994 (log-odds 7.38).

`kanm.bayes.ExactJointState` implements this exactly on the trust grid. `tests/test_exact_joint.py` checks it against full enumeration of the joint over `(p, r_1, r_2)`, checks order invariance over all permutations, and checks the published single-report equations.

### 3.2 Prior art (do not claim the diagnosis as new)

- **Merdes, von Sydow & Hahn (2021, *Synthese*)** state the core diagnosis. The Olsson model "assumes independence of hypothesis and reliability. But, on receipt of a report, these two variables become dependent"; a local sequential update "fails to factor in the emerging correlation between hypothesis and reliability", which "creates a dependence of the final state of the credences on the order in which reports arrive". They call it "only a naïve Bayesian agent, rather than the Bayes optimal model".
- **Hahn, Merdes & von Sydow (2018, *TopiCS*)** demonstrate the order effects by simulation.
- **Hahn, Merdes & von Sydow (2024)** and **Merdes (2025)** describe their agents as naïve Bayesian.

### 3.3 What appears not to be in the literature (Candidate)

The literature check found **no published exact alternative with Olsson's continuous trust densities**. Merdes et al. (2021) argue that "the only way" to avoid order effects is to remember all past reports and recompute from the initial priors, and that reliability learning in a global model is "arguably epiphenomenal".

The factorisation above shows a constant-memory exact filter exists. It is order invariant, and it still learns each source's reliability: the marginal `τ_i` changes as reports agree or disagree with the evolving credence.

This is a **candidate** contribution. It is a mathematical observation, and the literature search was targeted, not systematic. Before any claim of novelty:
1. Extend the search, in particular to Bovens & Hartmann (2003), Pallavicini et al. (2021) and Angere's unpublished derivation, none of which were read in full.
2. Respond explicitly to the "epiphenomenal" argument.
3. Remember that exactness is relative to the agent's assumed model. In a network, messages from different agents share upstream evidence, so neither rule is the Bayes-optimal response to the true data-generating process.

## 5. Reproduction attempt: Vallinder & Olsson (2014), Fig. 1

Script: `examples/reproduce_vallinder_olsson_2014.py`.
- **Setup:** 200 trials per cell, the published setup from V&O footnote 9 (no links, 20 agents, 100 steps, prior and activity uniform).
- **Not reported in the paper:** the trust-function shape. We ran Beta concentrations 4 and 16.
- **Table values:** V (Monte Carlo SE ≤ 0.006). The published values are approximate, read from the figure by our source check.
- **Provenance of the numbers:** generated with the final v0.3.0 code, after the D9–D10 discretisation fix, and re-confirmed with v0.4.0: a rerun with log-space trust weights (D16) agreed to 1e-16, and the later tie handling is never triggered in this design (instrumented check). A run made before that fix gave values higher by up to 0.026 at trust mean 0.99, where the old grid biased the prior mean; all cells with trust mean ≤ 0.8 changed by less than 0.001.

**Assessment.**
- **Reproduced (qualitatively):** V increases with R and with inquiry trust, flattens once trust exceeds about R, and is ordered by R.
- **Approximately reproduced:** the high-trust end at concentration 16: within 0.02 for R = 0.7 and 0.8, but 0.05 below the figure for R = 0.6.
- **Small decline at the top end:** at concentration 4, V falls from trust mean 0.9 to 0.99 for R = 0.6 and 0.7 (by about 0.012, roughly twice the per-cell SE; not tested formally). The published R = 0.6 curve is also slightly lower at E[τ] ≈ 1 than at 0.7 (0.38 vs 0.385). This is suggestive only.
- **Middle of the curve (trust 0.7):** 0.03–0.09 below the figure at concentration 16 and 0.14–0.21 below it at concentration 4.
- **Not reproduced:** the low-trust end (trust 0.52), which is about 4 times lower in KANM at concentration 16 (3.6–4.3×) and 7–9 times lower at concentration 4. The likely cause is the unreported trust-function shape. Laputa's default batch trust distributions are not Beta, and the V&O text percentages do not match their own figure either.
